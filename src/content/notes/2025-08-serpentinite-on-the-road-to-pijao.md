North of Pijao the road cuts through a rock that looks wrong for a coffee
landscape: blue-green, waxy, breaking into scaly plates. It is serpentinite —
ocean-floor mantle rock that was hydrated, sheared and stranded here when the
Cordillera Central was assembled.

It belongs to the **Rosario Complex**, and the reason it surfaces on this
road is the **Romeral fault system**. Between the Cauca–Almaguer fault to
the west and the Silvia–Pijao fault to the east runs a tectonic corridor of
serpentinites and schists, in fault contact with the volcano-sedimentary
rocks of Quebradagrande. The whole town sits inside it.

For the road this matters in a very practical way. The schists carry a
penetrative foliation, and in several cuts that foliation dips towards the
road at roughly the angle of the slope. A cut like that does not fail by
tumbling; it slides along the plane, sheet by sheet, every wet season. You
can see it in the annotated photograph: the pen lies on the plane that will
move next.

Of the 24 critical points on the corridor, the ones between 6 and 24 sit on
this rock, on slopes above 35 %. The fix is less about the wall and more
about the water: keep the foliation planes from saturating, and the sheets
stay put.
