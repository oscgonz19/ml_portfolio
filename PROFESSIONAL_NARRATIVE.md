## 3. Tone Guidelines

### Do:
- Lead with technical specifics (sensors, methods, outputs)
- Name the stack (Python, GeoPandas, Rasterio, PyTorch, FastAPI, Docker)
- Describe outcomes in operational terms ("prioritization", "risk ranking", "automated workflow")
- Use active, direct language ("I built", "designed", "shipped")

### Don't:
- Use corporate vagueness ("leveraged", "synergized", "drove innovation")
- Oversell ("revolutionized", "cutting-edge", "world-class")
- Hide behind team language when you did the work ("we" when you mean "I")
- List tools without context (just "Python, SQL, AWS" means nothing)

### Tone Examples

**Weak:**
> "Passionate data scientist with experience in geospatial analytics and machine learning."

**Strong:**
> "Geospatial ML Engineer. I build terrain risk models, satellite-derived analytics, and reproducible pipelines that ship — not notebooks that rot."

**Weak:**
> "Leveraged cutting-edge AI to drive insights for stakeholders."

**Strong:**
> "Built a landslide susceptibility engine using DEM derivatives, rainfall signals, and tree-based models. Output: ranked risk tiles used for corridor prioritization."

## 7. Project Descriptions (Pattern)

Use this structure for every project:

**[Title]**
> One sentence: what it is + what data + what output.

**What I built:**
> 2-3 bullet points on technical approach.

**Outcome:**
> What decision it supports or workflow it replaced.

### Example:
**Landslide Susceptibility Engine**
> End-to-end GeoAI pipeline predicting landslide susceptibility from terrain derivatives (slope, curvature, TWI), rainfall signals, and land cover.

**What I built:**
- Feature engineering from 30m DEM (14 terrain variables)
- XGBoost classifier with spatial cross-validation
- FastAPI endpoint serving risk tiles + interactive map

**Outcome:**
> Risk rankings used for municipal infrastructure prioritization — not a one-off map, a repeatable system.
